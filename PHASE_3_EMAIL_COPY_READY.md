## Email 2 — Water

**Beehiiv → Automation/Welcome sequence → Email 2**

**Subject:** Water filters: jug, under-sink or gravity?

**Preview text:** Narrow the format before comparing brands.

**Body:**

Water-filter shopping gets confusing when every system is presented as the answer.

Start with the format. A jug is inexpensive and simple but has limited capacity. Under-sink systems are convenient once installed. Gravity systems can suit households that want a countertop option without plumbing. Portable bottles solve a different problem again.

Then check replacement-filter cost, capacity and what you actually want the filter to address. The cheapest purchase price is not always the cheapest system to own.

## Email 3 — Healthy Home

**Subject:** A lower-tox home without throwing everything away

**Preview text:** Gradual, high-impact swaps beat a panic-buying spree.

**Body:**

A lower-tox home should not mean filling a skip with perfectly usable products.

Our approach is gradual: use up what is reasonable, then prioritise things you use frequently, breathe in, put on skin or use on clothing and bedding. Strongly fragranced laundry and cleaning products are often a practical place to review first.

The aim is lower unnecessary exposure with less waste and less stress — not a perfect house overnight.

**Subject:** The movement plan you can actually repeat

**Preview text:** Walking and simple strength before a cupboard full of equipment.

**Body:**

The most useful movement plan is the one you can still repeat next week.

Walking, basic strength work and regular movement through the day create a strong foundation without requiring expensive equipment. Add kit only when it makes the habit easier, safer or more progressive.

Consistency is deliberately less exciting than a new fitness gadget. It is also much harder to sell — which is why we keep putting it first.

## Email 5 — Nutrition

**Subject:** Food first. Supplements second.

**Preview text:** A simpler order for everyday nutrition.

**Body:**

Nutrition advice becomes complicated quickly. Wild & Well uses a simpler order.

Build repeatable meals around useful protein, fruit and vegetables, sensible carbohydrate sources and hydration. Convenience foods can help when they make good habits easier to maintain. Supplements come later, where there is a clear reason for them.

That does not make supplements automatically bad. It simply stops the supplement cupboard becoming the foundation of the diet.

## Email 6 — Practical resilience

**Subject:** Could your household manage 72 hours of disruption?

**Preview text:** Water, ordinary food, light, phone power and the boring essentials.

**Body:**

Practical resilience does not need to look like disaster preparation.

For a short disruption, think about drinking water, shelf-stable food your household already eats, a torch, phone charging, first-aid basics, hygiene and any household-specific essentials.

The useful question is not “How do I prepare for everything?” It is “What would become difficult first if normal services stopped for a day or two?” Start there.

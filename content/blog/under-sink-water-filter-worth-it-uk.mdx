Under-sink water filters appeal to people who want filtered drinking water without keeping a jug on the counter. But whether one is worth it depends on the problem you are actually trying to solve.

## When an under-sink filter can make sense

It may be a good fit if you:

- want filtered water available directly from a dedicated or existing tap;
- use enough drinking water that constantly refilling a jug is annoying;
- are comfortable with installation and ongoing cartridge changes;
- have checked that the system targets the substances you actually care about.

## What it will not necessarily solve

An under-sink drinking-water filter is not the same as a whole-home water softener. If your main frustration is limescale on taps, shower screens, kettles and appliances, read **[Shower Filter vs Water Softener UK](/blog/shower-filter-vs-water-softener-uk)** and our **[hard-water guide](/blog/hard-water-uk-myths-and-comfort)** first.

Likewise, not every under-sink system removes the same substances. Check the manufacturer's current reduction claims and independent certification where available rather than assuming 'filtered' means everything has been removed.

## Installation is part of the decision

Some systems connect inline to the cold-water feed, while others use a dedicated drinking-water tap. Reverse-osmosis systems can require more space and plumbing than simpler cartridge filters.

Before buying, check:

- cupboard space;
- connection requirements;
- whether a separate tap is needed;
- water pressure requirements;
- whether professional installation is recommended.

## Replacement filters matter more than the headline price

The purchase price is only the start. Compare the cartridge replacement interval, replacement cost and availability.

A cheaper system with expensive proprietary cartridges may cost more to own over several years than a more expensive unit with predictable replacement costs.

Read **[Water & Air Filter Replacement Costs UK](/blog/filter-replacement-costs-uk)** before choosing a system.

## Under-sink filter vs filter jug

A jug is cheaper and simpler to start with, but needs refilling and takes up fridge or counter space. An under-sink system costs more and requires installation, but can be more convenient for households using larger quantities of filtered drinking water.

For the full comparison, see **[Water Filter Jug vs Under-Sink Filter UK](/blog/water-filter-jug-vs-under-sink-filter-uk)**.

## What about reverse osmosis?

RO is a more intensive filtration approach and is not automatically the right answer for every household. Consider wastewater, installation, maintenance and whether the extra filtration is justified for your goal.

Our **[Waterdrop G3P600 RO guide](/best-reverse-osmosis-water-filter-uk-waterdrop-g3p600)** covers that decision in more detail.

## Quick decision rule

**Want simple, low-cost filtration:** start with a jug or countertop system.

**Want convenient everyday filtered drinking water:** an under-sink system may be worth comparing.

**Main problem is whole-home limescale:** investigate softening instead.

**Need high-intensity filtration:** compare RO carefully rather than assuming it is automatically better.

Choosing a bedroom dehumidifier by litre rating alone can be misleading. A **10L, 12L, 20L or 25L** label describes extraction capacity under test conditions; it does not automatically tell you which machine is right for your room.

## Start with the moisture problem

Ask what the dehumidifier is expected to do:

- control light overnight condensation;
- deal with persistent damp;
- help dry laundry indoors;
- serve one bedroom or several connected rooms.

The harder the workload, the more important capacity becomes.

## A smaller unit may be enough when

A modest-capacity dehumidifier can make sense for a single bedroom with a relatively light moisture load, particularly when doors are usually closed and you are not also asking the machine to dry washing.

Noise, physical size and energy use may matter more than buying the highest extraction rating available.

## A larger unit may make sense when

A higher-capacity model becomes more attractive when the moisture problem is persistent, the space is larger or more open, you want to use it across several rooms, or laundry drying is part of the job.

Larger capacity does not mean the machine must run at maximum effort all the time. A correctly sized unit may reach the target humidity more efficiently than an undersized machine that runs continuously.

## Check noise for bedroom use

A machine can be effective and still be a poor bedroom choice if you cannot tolerate its fan or compressor noise. Compare published noise figures, but also remember that the quietest mode may extract less water.

## Measure humidity instead of guessing

A simple hygrometer can help confirm whether humidity remains elevated and whether your changes are working. Do not buy a dehumidifier solely because windows occasionally mist up if the underlying issue can be addressed with ventilation or heating changes.

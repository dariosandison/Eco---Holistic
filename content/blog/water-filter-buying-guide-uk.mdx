The easiest way to overspend on water filtration is to compare products before deciding **what job the filter actually needs to do**. Start with the use case, then look at testing, replacement cost and how much maintenance you will realistically keep up with.

> **Fast route:** [Compare water-filter formats →](/best-water-filters-uk) • [See the maintained filtration shortlist →](/water-filtration-shortlist-uk)

## Start with the problem, not the product

Ask which of these best describes what you want:

| Your priority | Start by comparing | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Better-tasting everyday tap water with no installation | Jug or countertop | More frequent refilling and cartridge changes |
| Filtered kitchen water on demand | Under-sink carbon / multi-stage | Installation, space and ongoing filter cost |
| Higher-involvement broad filtration | Reverse osmosis (RO) | Installation, wastewater, space and replacement filters |
| Useful capacity without plumbing | Gravity system | Counter space, slower flow and cleaning |
| Travel, commuting or days out | Filter bottle | Smaller capacity and cartridge life |
| Shower-water filtration | Shower filter | Not the same as whole-home water softening |

## Jug and countertop filters

These are usually the lowest-friction starting point because there is little or no installation. They suit people who want filtered drinking water but do not want to alter plumbing.

Before buying, check:

- how much filtered water the household actually uses each day;
- the price and UK availability of replacement cartridges;
- the manufacturer’s specific testing or reduction claims;
- whether refilling and cleaning the unit will become annoying in normal use.

## Under-sink filtration

Under-sink systems make more sense when filtered water is used frequently and you want it available directly at the kitchen. The unit price is only part of the decision: installation, space and replacement filters are the long-term commitment.

Measure the available space and price at least a year of replacement filters before committing.

## Reverse osmosis (RO)

RO is a higher-involvement route and should solve a real need rather than simply sounding like the “most complete” option. Check the system’s installation requirements, wastewater ratio, flow, replacement schedule and total annual running cost.

For a worked example, see our **[Waterdrop G3P600 RO guide →](/best-reverse-osmosis-water-filter-uk-waterdrop-g3p600)**.

## Gravity filtration

Gravity systems can be useful when you want more capacity without plumbing. The trade-off is footprint, cleaning and slower processing. Make sure the system fits the space where you will actually keep and use it.

## Portable filter bottles

For commuting, travel and days away from home, a bottle can solve the problem with far less cost or installation than a household system. Compare capacity, flow and replacement-cartridge availability rather than choosing only on the bottle price.

## Four checks before spending money

### 1. Look for specific evidence

Prefer clear, checkable testing or certification information for the substances you care about. Broad “removes impurities” language tells you very little.

### 2. Calculate the replacement cost

A cheaper unit can become the expensive choice if cartridges cost more or need replacing often. Our **[filter replacement-cost guide →](/blog/filter-replacement-costs-uk)** explains what to include, and the **[water-filter running-cost calculator →](/tools/water-filter-running-cost-calculator)** lets you compare the annual cost of systems on the same basis.

### 3. Be realistic about maintenance

Every system works within a maintenance routine. If you know you will not clean a gravity unit or replace an under-sink cartridge on schedule, choose a lower-friction format.

### 4. Avoid buying more filtration than you need

A simple system that gets used and maintained is more useful than a complex one that becomes inconvenient.

Condensation on the inside of windows is common in UK homes, especially in colder months. It happens when warm, moisture-containing indoor air meets a cold surface and cools enough for water to condense.

That does **not** automatically mean you need to buy a dehumidifier.

## First: work out where the moisture is coming from

Common indoor moisture sources include:

- showers and baths;
- drying clothes indoors;
- cooking without effective extraction;
- several people sleeping in a closed bedroom;
- poor ventilation;
- leaks or building defects.

If condensation appears mainly after a shower or while cooking, source control and ventilation are the first places to look.

## Measure humidity before guessing

A simple hygrometer can help show whether indoor humidity is consistently high or whether the problem is limited to short spikes.

The useful question is not whether you can make the number as low as possible. It is whether moisture is staying elevated for long periods and contributing to persistent condensation, damp smells or mould growth.

## When ventilation may be enough

Try the free fixes first:

- use bathroom and kitchen extract fans properly;
- open trickle vents where fitted;
- air rooms when practical;
- avoid pushing large furniture tightly against cold external walls;
- reduce indoor clothes drying where possible.

If the condensation is occasional and clears quickly, a dehumidifier may be unnecessary.

## When a dehumidifier becomes more reasonable

A dehumidifier is more useful when moisture remains high despite sensible ventilation, when you regularly dry laundry indoors, or when the room has a persistent condensation workload that cannot easily be removed at source.

## Dehumidifier or air purifier?

A dehumidifier removes moisture from the air. An air purifier filters airborne particles. A purifier will not fix the moisture conditions that caused condensation.

For a direct comparison, see **[Air Purifier vs Dehumidifier for Mould UK](/blog/air-purifier-vs-dehumidifier-mould-uk)**.

## Quick decision rule

**Occasional condensation after showers or cooking:** improve extraction and ventilation first.

**Persistent condensation with consistently high humidity:** investigate the moisture source and consider a dehumidifier if source control is not enough.

**Water ingress, leaks or structural damp:** fix the building problem rather than relying on an appliance.

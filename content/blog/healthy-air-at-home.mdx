Healthy indoor air is usually less about buying one “perfect” device and more about solving the right problem in the right order.

> **Quick routes:** [Air Quality topic →](/topics/air-quality) • [Air Quality shortlist →](/air-quality-shortlist-uk) • [Dehumidifier guide →](/best-dehumidifiers-damp-mould-uk)

## Start with the problem, not the product

Most household air-quality complaints fall into four broad groups:

1. **Particles** — dust, pollen, smoke and fine cooking particles.
2. **Moisture** — condensation, damp and mould risk.
3. **Ventilation** — stale air, steam and pollutants building up indoors.
4. **Sources** — cooking, candles, sprays, fragranced products and other indoor emissions.

The useful question is: **which one is dominant in your home?**

## Quick decision table

| What you notice | First move | Product route if still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Dust, pollen, smoke particles | Reduce source + ventilate appropriately | Air purifier |
| Wet windows, musty rooms, high RH | Find moisture source + improve extraction | Dehumidifier |
| Cooking fumes | Use lids, extractor and ventilation | Purifier only as a secondary measure |
| Strong fragrance / spray exposure | Remove or reduce the source | Usually no device required first |
| Stale bedroom air | Improve ventilation pattern | Purifier only if particles are also a problem |

## 1. Control obvious sources first

This is the highest-value step because a machine should not be asked to compensate for avoidable pollution.

Useful examples:
- use pan lids and an effective extractor while cooking
- avoid unnecessary aerosol sprays
- reduce heavily fragranced products if they bother your household
- avoid routine indoor smoke sources where possible
- vacuum and dust regularly, particularly in bedrooms

Source control is usually cheaper and more effective than trying to filter everything after it enters the room.

## 2. Get ventilation right

Ventilation matters most after high-moisture or high-pollution activities such as cooking and showering.

A practical pattern is:
- use mechanical extraction while the activity is happening
- continue it for a period afterwards
- use short, purposeful window ventilation where appropriate

Leaving one window slightly open all day is not automatically better than targeted ventilation, especially in cold weather.

## 3. Measure humidity if damp or dryness is part of the problem

A simple hygrometer can prevent buying the wrong device.

If readings stay high and condensation is recurring, focus on moisture management. If readings stay very low, a dehumidifier would obviously be the wrong purchase.

## 4. When an air purifier makes sense

An air purifier is most relevant when **airborne particles** are the problem.

That can include:
- pollen
- household dust
- smoke particles
- traffic-related particulate pollution entering from outside
- particles generated by cooking

Before buying, check:
- room size
- stated CADR or equivalent airflow performance
- noise at the speed you will actually use
- replacement-filter cost
- whether filters are reliably stocked in the UK

If allergies are the main issue, start with the [Air purifiers for allergies guide →](/best-air-purifiers-allergies-uk).

For more choice, use the maintained [Air Quality shortlist →](/air-quality-shortlist-uk).

## 5. When a dehumidifier makes sense

A dehumidifier removes moisture; it does not remove the cause of a leak, repair poor ventilation or act as an air purifier.

It becomes more useful when:
- RH remains high for sustained periods
- condensation keeps returning
- indoor laundry drying is unavoidable
- ventilation improvements alone are not enough

Use the [Best dehumidifiers for damp & mould guide →](/best-dehumidifiers-damp-mould-uk) before comparing current products.

## 6. Replacement cost matters

Purifiers are filter-based products, so the purchase price is not the full cost.

Before buying, estimate:

**annual filter cost = filter price × replacements per year**

Then compare that with the machine price and how long you expect to own it.

Use the [Filter replacement-cost guide →](/blog/filter-replacement-costs-uk) before committing to a model with expensive or difficult-to-source filters.

## Room-by-room priorities

### Kitchen
Control cooking emissions at source first. Extraction and pan lids do more than relying on a purifier alone.

### Bathroom
Moisture is usually the priority. Use extraction and keep steam from spreading into the rest of the home.

### Bedroom
Focus on ventilation, dust control, bedding hygiene and any allergy triggers. A quiet purifier can make sense if airborne particles are a known problem.

### Living room
Think about candles, sprays, open windows near traffic, pets and general dust load before choosing equipment.

## The simplest buying rule

- **Particles problem?** Compare air purifiers.
- **Moisture problem?** Compare dehumidifiers.
- **Dry-air problem?** Measure first before considering humidification.
- **Source problem?** Remove or reduce the source before buying anything.

Damp and mould are not automatically a shopping problem. The useful sequence is **measure → identify the moisture source → improve ventilation or fix defects → then decide whether a dehumidifier is actually needed**.

## If you only do three things

1. **Measure humidity for seven days** in the bedroom and the room where the problem is worst.
2. **Reduce obvious moisture sources** such as indoor laundry, cooking steam and poorly vented showers.
3. **Document the pattern** with photos, dates and humidity readings, especially if you rent.

> Air purifiers do **not** remove moisture. If excess humidity is the driver, focus on moisture control and ventilation first.

## Step 1: Confirm there is a humidity pattern

A basic hygrometer is useful because it turns a vague feeling of “damp” into something you can track. Put one in the bedroom and another in the problem room, then record morning and evening readings for a week.

As a practical household guide, roughly **40–60% relative humidity** is commonly used as a comfortable range. Persistent readings above that range, especially alongside condensation and cold surfaces, are a stronger reason to investigate moisture control.

One high reading after a shower is less informative than repeated high readings across several days.

## Step 2: Work out where the moisture is coming from

### Indoor laundry

Drying clothes indoors can add a lot of moisture to the room. If possible, keep the drying room separate, use extraction or short periods of strong ventilation, and avoid spreading wet laundry around several rooms.

If indoor drying is unavoidable and humidity repeatedly stays high, a dehumidifier may become a practical tool rather than an impulse purchase.

### Bathroom moisture

Run extraction during showers and for a period afterwards. Keep steam from spreading through the home and wipe persistent condensation from windows or tiles while you investigate the wider ventilation pattern.

### Cooking

Use lids, extraction and short periods of ventilation after cooking. Steam that repeatedly condenses on cold surfaces can contribute to the same humidity load as laundry and showers.

### Leaks and building defects

A dehumidifier cannot repair a leak, failed seal, penetrating damp or other building defect. If there is an obvious water source or damage pattern, address or report that cause rather than using equipment to mask it.

## Step 3: Improve airflow without leaving the home cold all day

Short periods of effective ventilation can be more practical than leaving a window slightly open for hours. Where safe and suitable, use cross-ventilation, keep vents unobstructed and leave a little space between large furniture and cold external walls.

The goal is not simply “more air”; it is to help moisture leave the room instead of repeatedly condensing on the coldest surfaces.

## Step 4: Decide whether a dehumidifier is the right purchase

A dehumidifier becomes more relevant when:

- humidity stays elevated across repeated readings;
- condensation returns despite sensible ventilation changes;
- indoor laundry creates a recurring moisture load; or
- you need an active way to manage humidity while a longer-term ventilation issue is being addressed.

It is less useful as the first response when there is an unresolved leak or structural moisture source.

### Choose the machine by workload

Do not assume that the biggest extraction number is automatically the best choice. Consider room temperature, how many rooms you expect the unit to serve, whether laundry drying is a major use case, noise, drainage and likely running time.

## Step 5: Do not confuse humidity control with particle filtration

An air purifier is designed for airborne particles, not for removing water from the air. If pollen, dust or smoke is the main issue and your humidity is already normal, use the **[Air Quality Shortlist →](/air-quality-shortlist-uk)** to compare purifier routes instead.

If both problems exist, deal with them as two separate jobs: moisture control for humidity, filtration for airborne particles.

## Step 6: Clean small surface areas carefully

For small surface spots, improve the moisture conditions as well as cleaning the visible area. If mould returns quickly, that is a sign to keep looking for the moisture source rather than treating repeated cleaning as the solution.

People with respiratory conditions or significant sensitivities should take appropriate precautions and seek professional or clinical guidance where needed.

## Step 7: If you rent, make the pattern easy to evidence

Keep a simple record of:

- dated photos;
- humidity readings;
- where and when condensation appears;
- ventilation and laundry habits you have already changed;
- repair requests and responses.

When writing to the landlord or managing agent, describe what you have observed, attach the evidence and request the relevant inspection or repair rather than presenting the issue only as a cleaning problem.

## Quick decision checklist

- [ ] I have several days of humidity readings.
- [ ] I have checked showers, cooking and indoor laundry as moisture sources.
- [ ] I have checked for obvious leaks or defects.
- [ ] I have improved usable ventilation and airflow.
- [ ] Humidity or condensation still recurs.
- [ ] I know where a dehumidifier would run and what workload it needs to handle.

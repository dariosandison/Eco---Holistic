If you share your home with a cat or dog, an air purifier can help reduce **airborne particles** associated with pet dander and dust. But it works best as part of a broader cleaning and ventilation routine rather than as a standalone fix.

## Start with the room you actually use

Do not size a purifier to the whole home unless the manufacturer specifically supports that use. Choose the room where exposure matters most, such as a bedroom or living room, and compare the purifier's stated coverage with the actual room size.

A smaller purifier running continuously in the room you use most can be more useful than a larger unit that is rarely switched on.

## What filter should you look for?

For airborne pet-related particles, focus on a purifier with a particulate filter designed to capture fine particles. Check the manufacturer's current filter specification rather than relying on vague terms such as 'allergen technology'.

If odour is also a concern, look for a carbon stage as a separate feature. Particle filtration and odour reduction are different jobs.

## Cleaning still matters

An air purifier only treats air that passes through it. Pet hair and settled dust on floors, upholstery and bedding still need regular cleaning.

Useful basics include:

- vacuuming floors and soft furnishings;
- washing pet bedding;
- keeping the purifier intake and outlet unobstructed;
- ventilating the home when practical;
- reducing dust reservoirs where possible.

## Placement matters

Keep the purifier where air can circulate around it. Avoid wedging it tightly behind furniture or directly against a wall if the manufacturer's instructions require clearance.

For bedrooms, noise matters too. A purifier that is technically powerful but too loud to run overnight may be less useful than a quieter model you will actually use.

## Replacement filters are part of the price

Before buying, check how often the filter is expected to need replacement and what replacements cost. A low purchase price can become expensive if proprietary filters are costly or difficult to source.

Our **[Water & Air Filter Replacement Costs UK](/blog/filter-replacement-costs-uk)** guide explains why ownership cost matters.

## Air purifier or dehumidifier?

If your main issue is damp, condensation or mould growth, a purifier is not the first appliance to choose. Those are moisture problems.

See **[Air Purifier vs Dehumidifier for Mould UK](/blog/air-purifier-vs-dehumidifier-mould-uk)** if you are unsure which category fits your problem.

## Quick decision rule

**Pet dander and airborne dust:** compare particulate filtration, room size and replacement-filter cost.

**Pet odour:** check whether the purifier includes a meaningful carbon stage as well as particle filtration.

**Damp or condensation:** investigate moisture and ventilation instead of expecting a purifier to solve it.

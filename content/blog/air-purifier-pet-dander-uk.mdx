Pet allergy is not simply an allergy to visible dog or cat hair. [Allergy UK](https://www.allergyuk.org/resources/pet-allergy-factsheet/) explains that allergenic proteins can be present in dander, saliva and urine. These particles can become airborne or settle into bedding, carpets and furniture.

An air purifier may reduce some **airborne** particles in a room, but it will not remove settled allergens, clean soft furnishings or treat the allergy. Use it as one part of exposure reduction.

## Seek health advice when needed

If someone has wheezing, breathing difficulty, significant asthma symptoms or uncertainty about the trigger, use appropriate medical advice rather than relying on a new appliance. Severe breathing difficulty requires urgent help.

For ongoing symptoms, a GP or allergy professional can help distinguish pet allergy from pollen, dust mites, mould or another trigger.

## Start with exposure reduction

Allergy UK’s practical measures include keeping pets out of the bedroom, avoiding pets on soft furnishings and washing pet bedding regularly.

A realistic home routine is:

1. Make the main sleeping space dog-free when that is where symptoms matter most.
2. Wash pet bedding and washable covers regularly, following their care labels.
3. Vacuum floors and soft furnishings with suitable filtration.
4. Damp-dust rather than moving settled dust back into the air.
5. Groom and clean the dog appropriately, taking veterinary advice for skin problems.
6. Then assess whether room air filtration adds useful support.

## Hair, dander and odour are different jobs

| Problem | Primary response | What a purifier can do |
|---|---|---|
| Visible hair on floors and furniture | Vacuuming, washable covers and grooming | A pre-filter may catch some airborne hair, but it is not a vacuum |
| Airborne allergen-carrying particles | Exposure control plus particle filtration | A suitable particle filter can capture particles passing through it |
| Settled allergen reservoirs | Cleaning bedding, upholstery and surfaces | Cannot clean material the airflow does not reach |
| Pet odour | Hygiene, source control and ventilation | Carbon may help some odours; HEPA alone targets particles |
| Damp or condensation | Moisture source, extraction and humidity control | A purifier does not remove moisture |

## What to check in an air purifier

### Particle filtration

Look for a clearly specified replaceable particle or HEPA filter. Avoid treating vague “allergen technology” as a specification.

### Room sizing and airflow

Choose the actual room you are trying to improve. Record its dimensions and compare them with the manufacturer’s airflow or CADR basis. An “up to” room claim without an air-change assumption is difficult to compare.

### An accessible pre-filter

Dog hair and larger debris can load the first filtration stage. Check whether the pre-filter is accessible, how it is cleaned and whether doing so affects the warranty.

### Noise at a usable speed

For a bedroom or home office, compare noise at the speed likely to be used. A powerful unit switched off because it is intrusive provides no benefit.

### Replacement filters

Check the exact replacement model, current UK availability, price and expected interval. Heavy dust or pet hair can make maintenance especially important.

## Placement and operation

Follow the manufacturer’s clearance instructions and keep inlets and outlets away from curtains or furniture. Closing a purifier inside an unused room will not improve the room where exposure occurs.

Continuous, lower-speed operation may be more practical than occasional bursts, but follow the manufacturer’s instructions and consider electricity cost. Do not place cables where a dog can chew or trip over them.

## What about ionisers and ozone?

Do not seek ozone generation as a wellness feature. For a home allergy purchase, prioritise straightforward mechanical particle filtration, transparent performance data and maintainability.

## Air purifier or dehumidifier?

A dehumidifier is for excess moisture, condensation and damp conditions. It does not perform the same job as a particle filter. Some combination appliances include both functions, so inspect the specification carefully.

Read **[air purifier vs dehumidifier for mould UK](/blog/air-purifier-vs-dehumidifier-mould-uk)** when damp is part of the problem.

## A sensible decision rule

1. Confirm that pet allergen exposure is a plausible issue and seek medical advice for significant symptoms.
2. Create a dog-free bedroom and improve cleaning before buying equipment.
3. Choose the single room where filtration would be most useful.
4. Compare particle filtration, airflow, noise, pre-filter access and annual filter cost.
5. Review whether symptoms and household dust management actually improve; do not assume a purchase worked.

Bamboo grows quickly, which makes it attractive as a paper fibre. But the plant itself does not tell you the environmental impact of the finished toilet roll. **Fibre sourcing, pulping, bleaching, manufacturing, transport and packaging all matter.**

## The useful questions to ask

### Where did the fibre come from?
Look for meaningful sourcing information and recognised forestry or chain-of-custody certification where applicable. “Made with bamboo” tells you the fibre type, not the full sourcing story.

### Is it 100% bamboo or a blend?
Some products combine bamboo with other virgin or recycled fibres. That is not automatically bad, but the label should make the composition clear enough for you to compare products properly.

### How is it processed?
Turning a tough plant fibre into soft tissue requires industrial processing. Avoid assuming that bamboo means minimally processed. If a brand makes specific bleaching or chemical-processing claims, look for enough detail to understand what the claim actually covers.

### What about recycled toilet paper?
Recycled-fibre toilet paper avoids demand for new paper fibre and is worth comparing alongside bamboo. There is no need to treat bamboo as the default sustainable winner.

### Packaging and shipping
Plastic-free or reduced packaging can be a useful advantage, but do not let packaging dominate the whole comparison. A bulky product shipped long distances has other impacts too. Buying sensible quantities and avoiding waste matters regardless of fibre.

## Fragrance and lotions

For a simpler household product, unscented toilet paper without added lotions is an easy criterion. Fragrance is unnecessary for the basic job the product performs, particularly if someone in the household prefers fragrance-free products.

See our [fragrance-free home guidance](/topics/fragrance-free) for a wider approach that prioritises the products where fragrance exposure is easiest to reduce.

## Comfort still matters

A product that is technically impressive but uncomfortable enough that you immediately replace it is not a useful swap. Sheet size, ply, texture and how quickly a roll is used can change the real cost and practicality considerably.

## Bottom line

Bamboo can be a reasonable paper fibre, but **“bamboo” is not an environmental guarantee**. Compare transparent sourcing, fibre composition, processing claims, packaging, price and recycled alternatives rather than buying on the headline material alone.

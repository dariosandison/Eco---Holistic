A water filter jug and an under-sink filter can both improve drinking-water convenience, but they solve the problem in very different ways. The better choice depends less on which looks more premium and more on how much filtered water you use, whether you want installation, and what you actually want the filter to reduce.

## Choose a filter jug when simplicity matters most

A jug is usually the easier starting point when you want:

- no plumbing or installation;
- a lower upfront cost;
- something suitable for renting;
- a simple way to test whether filtered water improves taste or convenience for you.

The trade-offs are lower capacity, fridge or counter space, regular refilling and replacement cartridges.

## Choose an under-sink filter when you want higher capacity

An under-sink system can make more sense when:

- several people use filtered water every day;
- you do not want to refill a jug repeatedly;
- you are comfortable with installation;
- you want filtered water available directly from a tap.

The trade-offs are higher upfront cost, installation requirements, space under the sink and potentially more involved cartridge changes.

## Do they remove the same things?

Not automatically. The format does not tell you what the filter removes.

Before buying either type, check the manufacturer's specific reduction claims, independent certification where available, replacement schedule and whether performance depends on flow rate or water pressure.

Do not assume that a more expensive under-sink system necessarily addresses your particular concern better than a jug.

## What about hard water?

Neither category should automatically be treated as a whole-home water softener. If your main problem is limescale, first read **[Hard Water and Kettle Limescale in the UK](/blog/hard-water-kettle-limescale-uk)** and **[Hard Water UK: myths and comfort](/blog/hard-water-uk-myths-and-comfort)**.

## Compare ownership cost, not just purchase price

For a fair comparison, estimate:

- initial product price;
- cartridge cost;
- expected replacement frequency;
- installation cost, if any;
- how much filtered water your household realistically uses.

A cheap jug with frequent cartridge changes can add up, while an expensive under-sink system may be poor value if only one person uses a small amount of filtered water.

For a wider maintenance view, see **[Water and Air Filter Replacement Costs UK](/blog/filter-replacement-costs-uk)**.

## What if you want reverse osmosis?

Reverse-osmosis systems are a separate decision because they involve additional trade-offs such as installation, wastewater and membrane/filter maintenance. See **[Waterdrop G3P600 RO guide](/best-reverse-osmosis-water-filter-uk-waterdrop-g3p600)** before assuming RO is necessary.

## Quick decision rule

**Want the simplest, renter-friendly option?** Start with a jug or countertop format.

**Large household and tired of refilling?** Compare under-sink systems.

**Trying to solve whole-home limescale?** Investigate softening instead.

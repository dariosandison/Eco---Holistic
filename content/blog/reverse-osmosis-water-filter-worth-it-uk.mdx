Reverse osmosis (RO) is one of the more intensive household drinking-water filtration approaches. That does **not** mean every UK home needs it.

The right question is whether the additional filtration capability is useful enough for your situation to justify installation, maintenance and wastewater.

## What RO does

An RO system pushes water through a membrane designed to reject a broad range of dissolved substances. Systems commonly combine the membrane with pre-filters and sometimes post-filtration stages.

That makes RO fundamentally different from a simple carbon filter jug.

## Why people choose RO

Common reasons include wanting broad reduction of dissolved contaminants or having a specific water-quality concern that a simpler filter does not address.

But broad filtration is not automatically a reason to buy the most complicated system available.

## The trade-offs

Before choosing RO, consider:

- installation space beneath the sink;
- filter and membrane replacement;
- wastewater produced during filtration;
- flow rate and storage arrangement, depending on system design;
- purchase and ongoing maintenance cost.

If you are unwilling to maintain the system properly, a simpler filtration route may be the better real-world choice.

## RO is not a water softener

An under-sink drinking-water RO system should not be confused with whole-home water softening.

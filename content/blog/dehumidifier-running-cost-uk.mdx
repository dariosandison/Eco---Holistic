When comparing dehumidifiers, the obvious question is often: **how much will it cost to run?**

The answer depends on more than the wattage printed on the product page. Runtime, room conditions, capacity and humidity control all matter.

## The basic cost calculation

To estimate electricity cost, use:

**power in kilowatts × hours used × your electricity unit rate**

For example, a 250 W appliance is 0.25 kW. If it ran continuously for four hours, it would use about 1 kWh before allowing for cycling or automatic control.

Your actual cost depends on your current tariff, so use the unit rate shown by your energy supplier rather than an old national average.

## Why lower wattage is not automatically cheaper

A smaller machine may draw less power while running but need to run for much longer if it is undersized for the moisture load.

A larger unit may use more power at a given moment but remove moisture faster and cycle off sooner.

This is why **capacity and workload** matter as much as headline wattage.

## Four things to compare

### 1. Moisture load

Drying laundry indoors, persistent condensation and several damp rooms create a larger workload than occasional bedroom condensation.

### 2. Rated extraction capacity

Compare the litres-per-day rating, but remember that laboratory ratings are usually measured under controlled conditions. Real UK home performance varies with temperature and humidity.

### 3. Humidistat and automatic control

A machine that can maintain a target humidity and cycle rather than run unnecessarily can be easier to manage than one you simply leave running continuously.

### 4. Where you will use it

Running one appropriately sized machine in the area that needs it can make more sense than moving an undersized unit around the house all day.

## Do not ignore purchase price and lifespan

Running cost is only one part of ownership. Also compare:

- purchase price;
- warranty;
- filter cleaning or replacement requirements;
- noise;
- expected usage pattern;
- whether the unit is appropriately sized.

A cheap-to-run machine that is too weak to solve the problem is not necessarily good value.

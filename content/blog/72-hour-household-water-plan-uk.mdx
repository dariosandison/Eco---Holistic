A short water interruption is inconvenient. A longer one quickly becomes a household problem because drinking, cooking and basic hygiene all depend on the same supply.

This guide is deliberately practical. It is not about predicting disasters. It is about giving a UK household a sensible buffer for temporary disruption — whether that comes from maintenance, severe weather, infrastructure failure or another local emergency.

## The simple starting point: 3 litres per person, per day

For drinking and basic food preparation, a useful minimum planning figure is **3 litres per person per day**.

For 72 hours, that means:

| Household size | Minimum drinking/cooking water for 3 days |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 person | 9 litres |
| 2 people | 18 litres |
| 3 people | 27 litres |
| 4 people | 36 litres |
| 5 people | 45 litres |

That is a minimum rather than a luxurious allowance. Hot weather, illness, pregnancy, breastfeeding and strenuous activity can all increase needs.

## Storage comes before filtration

If the mains supply stops completely, a filter attached to your tap cannot create water that is not there.

So the first layer of resilience is boring but effective: **keep some potable water stored at home**.

Good habits include:

- Keep drinking water in food-safe containers intended for water storage.
- Store it somewhere cool, dark and away from chemicals, fuels or strong odours.
- Rotate commercially bottled water according to its date and packaging condition.
- If you fill reusable containers with tap water, keep the containers scrupulously clean and refresh the water regularly.
- Do not rely on an open bucket or improvised container for long-term drinking-water storage.

## Where filtration fits

Filtration is useful, but different products solve different problems.

### Everyday household filters

Jug, under-sink and countertop systems are mainly useful for improving normal household drinking water and, depending on the specific system and verified claims, reducing selected substances.

Before buying, check exactly **what the manufacturer says the filter has been tested to reduce**, how often cartridges need replacing and what those replacements cost.

If you want a normal everyday household system, see our [UK water-filter shortlist](/best-water-filters-uk).

### Portable filters

Portable filter bottles are useful when travelling, hiking or when you need a compact way to treat water away from your normal kitchen setup.

The important point is that **a portable filter is not permission to drink from any unknown source**. Performance varies by product. Check the manufacturer's verified claims and instructions, and avoid obviously polluted water where possible.

## What a filter may not solve

No single consumer filter should be assumed to make every contaminated water source safe.

A product designed to improve taste or reduce chlorine is not automatically suitable for microbiologically unsafe water. Likewise, a filter tested for bacteria or protozoa may not remove every chemical contaminant, dissolved salt or heavy metal.

If an official water-quality incident occurs, follow the instructions from your water company and local authorities. A **boil-water notice**, a **do-not-drink notice** and a total supply outage are different problems and require different responses.

## A sensible 72-hour household setup

You do not need an expensive survival kit. A useful basic setup is:

1. **Enough stored drinking water for three days.**
2. **A clean refillable bottle for each household member.**
3. **A normal household filter** if you already prefer filtered tap water.
4. **A portable filtration option** if you travel, hike or want an additional backup layer.
5. **A kettle or safe alternative way to boil water** if mains electricity is unavailable and official advice requires boiling.
6. **A written note of your water supplier's emergency contact details**, so you are not completely dependent on a working phone or internet connection.

## If the tap stops unexpectedly

First, work out whether the problem is inside your property or affecting the local supply.

- Check another cold tap.
- Check whether neighbours are affected.
- Look for an outage notice from your water company if communications are working.
- Protect the water you already have: avoid unnecessary flushing, washing and cleaning until you understand the situation.
- Use stored drinking water for drinking and food preparation first.

If the interruption is prolonged, your supplier or local authority may arrange alternative supplies. Follow official advice rather than improvising with questionable water sources unnecessarily.

## Waterdrop vs Water-to-Go: different jobs

For Wild & Well readers, these two types of product solve very different problems.

**Waterdrop-style under-sink filtration** is about normal daily convenience: filtered water from your kitchen tap, with replacement cartridges as the main ongoing cost.

**Water-to-Go-style portable filtration** is about portability: travel, walking, outdoor use and having a compact secondary option away from the kitchen.

Neither replaces the need to store some water if your household wants genuine short-term resilience.

## The Wild & Well rule

Start with the free, boring measures before buying equipment.

Store some water. Know who supplies your water. Understand what type of filter you own. Replace cartridges on time. Know that different incidents require different responses.

That gives you far more practical resilience than buying a cupboard full of gadgets without understanding what they do.

The purchase price is only part of an air purifier's cost. Before buying one, check **how much replacement filters cost and whether you can reliably obtain them in the UK**.

## Calculate annual filter cost

A simple comparison is:

**replacement-filter price × expected replacements per year**

Treat the manufacturer's replacement interval as guidance rather than a guaranteed lifespan. Actual loading depends on use, pollution levels, pets, smoke and operating time.

## A cheap purifier can become expensive

An inexpensive machine with costly proprietary filters can cost more over several years than a more expensive purifier with reasonably priced consumables.

This is particularly important if you plan to run the purifier every day.

## Check availability, not just price

A filter that costs £30 is not useful if it becomes unavailable. Before committing to a purifier, check whether genuine replacement filters are sold consistently in the UK and whether the model appears to be part of an established product line.

## Do not stretch filters indefinitely

Running a heavily loaded filter far beyond its recommended service period undermines the reason you bought the purifier. Budget for maintenance before purchase rather than treating it as an unexpected cost later.

## Include electricity, but keep perspective

Power consumption matters, particularly for equipment running many hours per day, but filter replacement can be the less obvious recurring cost. Compare both rather than focusing on wattage alone.

## Compare the whole ownership proposition

Before buying, write down:

- purifier purchase price;
- replacement-filter price;
- estimated replacement frequency;
- UK filter availability;
- noise at the speed you expect to use;
- power consumption;
- warranty and expected service life.

Then compare like with like.

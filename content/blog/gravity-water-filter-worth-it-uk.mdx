A gravity water filter sits in an interesting middle ground: more capacity than a typical filter jug, but without the plumbing required by many under-sink systems.

Whether that makes one worthwhile depends on **why you want filtered water and how much you use**.

## How gravity filters work

Water is poured into an upper chamber and moves through the filter using gravity rather than mains pressure or electricity. Filtered water collects in a lower chamber ready for use.

That simplicity is one of the format's main attractions.

## When gravity filtration makes sense

It may suit households that:

- want a larger stored volume than a jug;
- do not want permanent plumbing changes;
- are comfortable filling the system manually;
- value a filtration method that does not rely on electricity;
- want one filtration option that can also fit a modest household-resilience plan.

## The disadvantages

Gravity systems take up counter or stand space, need manual refilling and can be slower than opening a dedicated filtered-water tap.

They also still depend on replacement filters. Check price, service life and UK availability before buying.

## Do not buy on format alone

The fact that a filter is gravity-fed does not tell you what it removes. Compare the manufacturer's specific reduction claims and any relevant independent testing.

If a seller makes very broad claims without useful evidence, treat that cautiously.

## Gravity filter vs jug

Choose a jug when low upfront cost, fridge storage and small volumes matter most. Gravity systems become more attractive when you want more capacity and are happy to dedicate space to the unit.

For a wider format comparison, read **[Water Filter Jug vs Under-Sink Filter UK](/blog/water-filter-jug-vs-under-sink-uk)**.

## Gravity filter vs under-sink

An under-sink system usually wins for convenience because filtered water is available from a tap. Gravity wins when avoiding plumbing, portability or non-electric operation matters more.
